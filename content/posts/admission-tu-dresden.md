## Before the application

I'd shortlisted TU Dresden alongside a few other technical universities
mainly because the program matched what I'd studied for my undergrad, and
the application requirements looked less document-heavy than some of the
other shortlists I'd built. Before starting the actual application, I spent
a couple of weeks getting a rough transcript evaluation done and lining up
recommendation letters — both of which took longer than I expected, mostly
because I was chasing signatures from professors who were traveling.

## The application itself

The application went through the university's own portal rather than
uni-assist, which was a relief — one fewer intermediary, one fewer set of
fees. I uploaded scanned transcripts, a statement of purpose, my language
certificate, and CV. The portal flagged missing fields immediately, which
was useful, but the file-size limits on scanned documents meant I had to
recompress a couple of PDFs before they'd upload.

## The wait

After submitting, there wasn't much to do but wait. I checked the portal
status page more often than was useful. Somewhere around six weeks later a
status change appeared — not a full decision yet, just a note that the
application had moved to "under review." I mention this mainly because I
didn't find much written about that in-between status when I was searching,
and it caused a fair amount of unnecessary anxiety.

## The offer

The admission letter, when it came, was conditional — pending final
transcripts and proof of my bachelor's degree completion, both of which I'd
flagged as "in progress" at the time of application. Uploading the final
documents once my results were out closed out the conditions within about
two weeks.

From here, the next step was the blocked account and visa appointment —
covered in a separate post from the same application cycle.
